Case Study 01 / Enterprise AI Infrastructure

From Chat UI to Enterprise AI Platform.

A high-level account of integrating OpenWebUI with corporate identity and a centralized model-request path in an enterprise environment.

Challenge

Making AI models available to employees was not simply a matter of installing a chat interface. The platform had to connect to existing organizational identities, present a consistent multi-model experience, and be managed as an internal service.

What I worked on

My contributions included deploying an enterprise OpenWebUI platform, integrating corporate sign-in through Active Directory and Keycloak, and connecting model requests to a centralized Token Manager. The architecture below focuses on integration decisions; proprietary implementation details are omitted.

Engineering decisions & trade-offs

Problem

Native LDAP authentication was unreliable in the original deployment environment.

Decision

Introduce Keycloak as the intermediary identity integration layer between Active Directory and OpenWebUI.

Trade-off

Keycloak introduced an additional identity dependency requiring operation, security maintenance, and monitoring.

Model access

Route OpenWebUI requests through a centralized Token Manager instead of direct provider integration.

Outcome

The platform was deployed with organizational identity integration and a centralized model-request path. Approximately 250 accounts were provisioned, with access to a dynamic model catalog containing about 450 entries at one point in time. No active-use measurement, performance improvement, or cost-saving estimate is claimed here without supporting evidence.

The engineering value of an enterprise AI platform comes from reliable integrations and understood operational trade-offs—not simply from the number of models it lists.
